# KL-014 — decision, 2026-08-13

v0.4's primary endpoint was the regime distribution, registered because it
settles whether the unit-of-claim gap matters. It was measured. It does not,
much. This records the measurement, the decision it forces, and what was done
instead.

## The measurement

OpenAlex, works published 2015–2024, n = 106,711,630.

| regime | proxy | share |
|---|---|---|
| bundled-derivative | `type:review` | 0.4% |
| bundled-derivative | "review" or "meta-analysis" in title | **~2.1%** |
| bundled-independent | "Many Labs" replication | 0.004% |
| retracted | `is_retracted` | 0.089% |

`type:review` is unreliable — it returns 460,258 where 2,015,641 works carry
"review" in the title, an undercount of roughly 4×. The title proxy is used as
the upper estimate, so ~2% is the generous reading of how much of the literature
is bundled-derivative.

Against that: the v0.2 pilot found **5 of 9 verified real claims (56%) cite no
resolvable primary source at all.**

## The decision

**The unit-of-claim gap is second-order. The attribution gap is first-order.**
They differ by more than an order of magnitude, and the conclusion survives the
measurement error — even if the review count is undercounted tenfold, 20% is
still below 56%, and the honest estimate is 2%.

Consequences, taken now rather than deferred:

- **U-A-artifact is adopted as the working unit rule.** One publication is one
  claim. It is wrong for ~2% of works, and that is an acceptable approximation
  at current corpus composition.
- **v0.4 is deprioritised, not withdrawn.** The four candidate rules and the
  R1.4 unit-denomination consequence remain registered and pinned. If corpus
  composition shifts, or if a deployment ingests a bundled-heavy corpus, the
  rule is already written and can be run without being re-derived.
- **Effort moved to attribution**, which is where the measurement says the
  leverage is.

Registering v0.3 and v0.4 was, in hindsight, a detour of two versions. It was a
detour that produced the R1.4 unit-denomination finding and the three-regime
taxonomy, so it was not wasted — but the endpoint that would have caught it
earlier is the one measured here, and it could have been measured first.

## What was done instead

`EvidenceGraph(require_root_evidence=True)` — a parentless claim carrying no
evidence is refused (`UnattributedRootError`), or in permissive mode recorded as
an `unattributed_root` violation with `immunity_applicable` set False.

Why this is the right lever, and not more mathematics: an unattributed claim is
a root, roots are what `margin` counts, and every theorem in `formal/lean/` is
already correct about that. Nothing in the formalism is wrong. The gap is that
the ingest layer mints roots for claims that identify nothing. That is a policy
question with a one-line answer, and it needs no new theorem.

Off by default — it is a semantic tightening and existing callers have not been
migrated. It should become the default once they have.

## What this does NOT settle

- HRI-1 is still unanswered. The split factor has not been measured on any
  corpus that could bear it.
- KL-015 / HRI-2 remains blocked through its declared dependency, and the block
  is now permanent rather than pending: adopting U-A does not validate a root
  criterion against known-independent observations, which is what v0.3 was for
  and could not supply.
- The gate refuses claims with empty evidence. It does not check that the
  evidence *resolves* to anything. A claim carrying `{"source": "trust me"}`
  passes. Verifying resolvability is a larger piece of work and is not attempted.
