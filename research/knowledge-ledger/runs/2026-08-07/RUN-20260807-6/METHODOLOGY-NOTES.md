# Methodology notes — RUN-20260807-6

M1–M23 in force. Two additions.

## M24 — An expectation over an object enumerates the members it covers; concepts are not quantifiers

FC1's E4 registered "identical in every structural field (conclusion, all of
`search`, all of `evidence`)" for worlds related by renaming — and `evidence`
contains two members that *echo declared names*, so the expectation was
unsatisfiable as written and failed on first execution (REG-101). The word
"structural" did the damage: it named a concept and let the reader (this
run, minutes later) believe the field list beneath it agreed. Rule: an
expectation over a compound object lists the members it covers by name, and
any member excluded is excluded by name with the reason. This is M15/H1's
lesson moved one step earlier — from verifying claims to writing them.

## M25 — "Discharged by prerequisite" is a result, and it is recorded as one

KL-001's named first check turned out to be KL-000's I2 in repository
costume: the check passed, produced no new evidence, and its real content
was the *discovery that no new evidence was possible* from that check with
the existing components. The record treats this as a first-class outcome —
verdict (a), preregistered as a possibility before execution, with the
demonstration (name-free receipt identity with an enumerated world) rather
than an assertion. Two disciplines made it cheap and honest: registering
both verdicts in advance so (a) could not be dressed up as more, and paying
four synthetic worlds to *measure* the redundancy instead of arguing it.
The same pattern discharged KL-011's blocker in the same run: in both cases
the correction cites the discharging evidence and preserves the superseded
text, because a record that silently loses its history of being wrong will
be trusted exactly once.
