My brief said the backlog was missed because it "has no natural trigger."
That was speculation and it is not what happened. The cause is now evidenced

THE EVIDENCE

RUN-20260807-1's instructions came from the master-loop prompt, which
enumerates the closing packet by name: DRAFT-RUN-REPORT, CONSTRAINTS,
RESEARCH-BACKLOG, KERNEL-STATUS-SNAPSHOT, NEXT-RUN-PROPOSAL, HANDOFF -- with
what each must contain.

RUN-4, RUN-5 and RUN-6 were driven by operator briefs I wrote. All three say
only "Close with the full versioned packet." None of them names
RESEARCH-BACKLOG anywhere. Grep them: the string does not appear.

The outcome tracks the instruction exactly:
  RUN-1..3  enumerated list in context   6 of 6 artifacts
  RUN-4     concept only, list still in context   6 of 6
  RUN-5     concept only                  backlog absent
  RUN-6     concept only                  backlog absent

Neither RUN-5 nor RUN-6 names RESEARCH-BACKLOG in any file, including their
manifests. It was not attempted and dropped. It was never in their
instructions, because I replaced an enumeration with a concept.

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR YOUR OUTPUT

This is M24 -- your own note from RUN-6: "an expectation over an object
enumerates the members it covers; concepts are not quantifiers." I wrote "the
full versioned packet" and meant six named files. The defect you diagnosed in
your own failed E4 was, in the same run, present in the instructions you were
given.

Record the cause as this, not as a missing trigger. And the mechanical fix is
two-sided, not one:

  1. the close verifies every required artifact is present and fails if not
     (as briefed);
  2. a brief that requires a packet ENUMERATES it. An operator instruction
     that names a concept where the protocol names members is the same defect
     one layer up, and it is the layer that actually caused this.

State (2) as a requirement on operator briefs, not as advice. If a future
brief says "the full packet" without naming the members, that brief is
defective and the run should say so before executing it.
